Your Rights

Many people are unaware that airlines operating in New Zealand are bound by a specific set of regulations, which take precedence over any terms and conditions listed on their tickets. The New Zealand Civil Aviation Act 1990 clearly outlines the rights of consumers in cases of flight delays or cancellations.

The New Zealand Civil Aviation Act

Your Rights Under the New Zealand Civil Aviation Act 1990

Where your domestic flight has been delayed or cancelled, you may be entitled to claim the lesser of:

  • the amount of damage proved to have been sustained as a result of the delay; or
  • an amount representing 10 times the sum paid for the carriage.

You may also be entitled to a remedy under the New Zealand Consumer Guarantees Act 1993.

Reasons for Delay

Determining whether a delay or cancellation is within the airline's control can be challenging. For instance, Jetstar's compensation and refunds section on their website provides the following examples:

Delays or cancellations within its control include engineering problems, IT system outages, delayed baggage delivery due to staffing issues, late cleaning or loading of catering onto the aircraft, crew or staffing issues, and overbooking.

Delays or cancellations outside its control include weather events, air traffic control issues, industrial action by third parties, and security issues.

Essentially, this means that the compensation for delays or cancellations could be as much as ten times the cost of your ticket. For example, if your ticket cost $100, the maximum compensation you could receive would be $1000. This amount is significantly higher than what airlines typically offer.

For additional information, you can also visit the Air New Zealand's domestic passenger rights page and the consumerprotection.govt.nz page.
